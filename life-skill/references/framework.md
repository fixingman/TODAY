# Working framework

The four stage names and four candidate problem statements below are PROVISIONAL PACKAGE DESIGN, not recovered wording from the original chat. Confirm or replace when the source becomes available. The requested three lens names are user supplied; their definitions here are operational suggestions.

## Four stages

1. **Observe and explore** — collect concrete moments of energy, interest, flatness, friction, satisfaction, avoidance, or surprise. Ask “What happened?” before “What does it mean?” Output: observations and open questions, not a diagnosis.
2. **Frame candidate problems** — distinguish situations, interpretations, needs, constraints, and possible mechanisms. Compare candidates through the three lenses and counterevidence. Output: a revisable problem map and a chosen question worth learning about.
3. **Design and run small experiments** — choose a low-cost action that can distinguish explanations, with an effort budget, observable signal, and review point. Output: an experiment record and optional approved to-do action.
4. **Review and integrate** — compare prediction with experience, include noncompletion and resistance, revise the map, and choose what to keep or change. Output: learning and a next question, experiment, or pause.

These stages form a loop. Return to observation or framing whenever the current explanation stops fitting. Do not make introspection a prerequisite for action.

## Three lenses

| Lens | Working question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | What feels worth moving toward, and why? | Recurring values, interests, tradeoffs, desired contributions |
| Engagement | When do I feel absorbed, alive, connected, or willing to return? | Concrete experiences before/during/after activities, contexts, relationships |
| Leverage | Where might a feasible change make a meaningful difference? | Constraints, effort, dependencies, environmental changes, repeatable effects |

Do not collapse these into a productivity score. They can disagree: an activity may be meaningful yet draining, absorbing yet directionless, or useful without being personally engaging.

## Four provisional candidate problems

All are hypotheses to confirm, replace, combine, or reject. They are not claims about Can.

- **P1 — Unclear direction:** “I may have difficulty identifying which possibilities feel worth pursuing.” Explore competing values and concrete moments of meaning; do not assume a single life purpose must be found.
- **P2 — Limited engagement:** “My current activities may provide too little sustained interest, vitality, or connection.” Compare contexts and before/during/after experience; do not infer a clinical condition.
- **P3 — Difficulty turning insight into action:** “I may understand possibilities but struggle to translate them into manageable experiments.” Compare action size, ambiguity, environment, and available support rather than labeling motivation.
- **P4 — Effort concentrated in low-leverage places:** “I may be investing effort in changes that do not address the constraint that matters most.” Explore whether a smaller environmental or structural change shifts experience; do not equate leverage with output.

For every candidate preserve: user wording, provenance, endorsement status, supporting observations, counterevidence, uncertainty, alternatives, and next discriminating question. These four are a starting map, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a ranked assessment.

## Conversation examples

Observation: “I forgot the time while helping a friend think through a project.”
Response: “That sounds like a useful engagement signal. Was it the problem itself, helping your friend, or thinking together that held your attention?” Capture the original words; keep all three interpretations open.

Review: “I never did the experiment.”
Response: “What got in the way, if anything stands out?” Record noncompletion and known context. Consider simplifying, changing the setting, or stopping; do not infer fear or resistance without evidence.

Therapist handoff: “I noticed a recurring pattern of enjoying collaboration but withdrawing before arranging it. I would like to explore what that withdrawal means to me.” Mark this as a draft for the user to edit, not an explanation of their motives.
